# Photo check: Top-outs (a), the heel on the lip

Simon twice found the heel-hook leg in Top-outs (a) wrong. These notes set the two drawn attempts beside photographs and video stills of a heel hooked on a boulder's lip. The photos were reference only and stay out of the repository. They're saved in the session scratchpad (`topout-photos/`), with a contact sheet of the best six in `topout-photos/contact.png`.

## What the photos agree on

The moment is a climber hanging from the lip with both hands and one heel hooked on the lip out to the side. The photos agree on the following points.

- **The heel** is 1–2 forearm lengths (about 40–70 cm) outside the near hand. It sits between hand height and a forearm below the hands, so at about head to shoulder height. The back and bottom of the heel rest on top of the lip, just over the edge. Nothing hooks under it.
- **The leg** is long and nearly straight. The knee is at 150–170° and the line from hip to heel rises 20–40° out to the side.
- **The knee** points up, and a little back towards a viewer behind the climber. Where the leg bends, the knee is *above* the straight line from hip to heel, so the leg forms a shallow upside-down V. No photo has the knee below that line.
- **The shin** carries on the thigh's line out to the heel. Only later in the move, as the climber rocks over, does the knee rise high and the shin drop from it to the heel (photos 4, 6, 7 and 10 below).
- **The foot** is pointed, with the ankle at about 115–150°. It carries on the shin's line with the toes pointing out to the side and 10–30° up. From behind or from the side you see the outside of the shoe, as the earlier notes said. The foot is never flexed to a right angle with its sole flat along the lip.
- **The hips** are close to the wall, a torso length or so below the lip, with the arms straight or nearly straight. They're square to the wall or open a little towards the heel. The torso hangs about upright and leans a little towards the heel.

This agrees with the earlier notes (photo-check-dynamic-terrain-concepts-considerations.md, top-out row, and photo-check-figs_feet.md, heel-hook rows): a "fairly long leg", with the foot pointed and the toes out to the side and only a little up.

The anatomy agrees too. A leg lifted out to the side and turned out so the knee points up shows the outside of the shoe to someone behind, with the toes up. Pointing the foot then swings the toes out along the leg. If the knee pointed at the ground the leg would have to be turned in hard, and no photo shows that.

## The two drawings against the photos

**Second edition** (shin up and out to the heel, foot pointed in line with it). This is the closer of the two. The pointed foot in line with the shin and the heel out at the lip match the photos. What's wrong is the knee. The thigh runs nearly level from the hip and the knee sits *below* the line from hip to heel, with the shin rising steeply (about 60°) from it. That reads as a knee bent the wrong way. The shoe also rises into the air from the edge, so only the tip of the heel touches the lip, where in the photos the heel rests on top of it.

**Redraw** (thigh out to the side, knee down and out, shin rising to the heel, foot at a right angle). This is further off. The knee drops lower still, the shin is almost vertical and the foot is flexed to 90° with its sole flat along the lip. That reads as a foot standing on the lip, not a heel hooked over it. To get the shin vertical under the heel the knee would have to point at the ground, and no photo shows that.

**For the next drawing.** Draw one nearly straight line from the hip to the heel, rising about 30–40°. In the figure the heel stays where it is now, at the lip about two head-widths outside the right hand. The hip-to-heel distance there is close to a full leg length, so a nearly straight leg fits as drawn. Bend the knee only 10–20°, with the knee a little *above* the line. Point the foot at about 130–150° so that it carries on the shin, toes out to the side and a little up. Show the outside of the shoe, and rest the back and bottom of the heel on top of the lip, just over the edge.

## Readings, photo by photo

View: where the camera is relative to the climber. "Heel" gives its position against the hands and the lip. Angles are read by eye off the picture and are good to about ±15°.

### Heel on the lip of a top-out or mantle

1. **climbinghouse.com, mantle article** (`climbinghouse.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/bouldering_mantle.png`, page climbinghouse.com/how-to-mantle-technique/). Outdoor sandstone, from behind and to the climber's left side, with her back and shoulder in view. This is the moment itself: both hands on the lip, arms nearly straight.
   - Heel: right heel far outside the right hand, about 2½ forearm lengths. It's about a forearm below the hands, at head height, on the rounded top of the lip.
   - Thigh: rises from the hip out and up about 20°. The knee is about level with the head, well below the hands, and points up and back.
   - Shin: carries on almost level out to the heel. Knee about 165°.
   - Foot: strongly pointed, ankle about 150°, toes out along the leg and about 25° up. The back and bottom of the heel touch the rock and the outside of the shoe, with its logo, faces the camera.
   - Hips and torso: hips close to the wall and a little open to the heel side; the torso hangs upright.
2. **climber.co.uk, topping-out skills page** (`climber.co.uk/images/Skills/6-Topping-Out-A.jpg`). Fontainebleau-style rounded top, from in front, to the side and above. The moment itself: both hands on the rounded top, the body hanging below.
   - Heel: right heel about a forearm outside the lower (right) hand. It's at about hand height and over the lip, on the top surface.
   - Thigh: rises out and up about 50° from the hip. The knee is below the lip and points up and out, towards the camera.
   - Shin: carries on the same line to the heel. The leg is nearly straight, knee about 160°.
   - Foot: pointed, ankle about 140–150°, toes up and out along the leg. The heel's back and bottom are on the top and the strap side of the shoe faces the camera.
   - Hips and torso: hips about a torso length below the lip and away from the rock, as the face below is undercut. The torso leans back.
3. **climbing.com, "learn this: master topouts", heel rock-over frames 1–4** (`cdn.climbing.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/heel-rockover-1.jpg` to `-4.jpg`). Outdoor granite, from above and in front, on top of the boulder.
   - Frame 1 is the moment itself. Both hands are on the top and the right heel is on the lip about 1½ forearms outside the right hand, at hand height. The leg runs nearly level along the lip with the knee only slightly bent, and the toes point up and out.
   - Frame 2: the knee has come up above the lip and points up and out. The thigh rises from the hip and the shin drops to the heel on the top, knee about 100°.
   - Frames 3–4: the hips come over the heel, the foot turns to stand on the top and the inside hand presses.
4. **climbinghouse.com, mantle article** (`climbinghouse.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/climber_mantling_boulder.png`). Font-style flat-topped boulder, from behind and a little below. This is later than our moment: the chest and the right hand are over the lip.
   - Heel and foot: the left foot is on the top surface about a forearm and a half outside the body. The foot is pointed, with the toes out to the left and a little down.
   - Leg: the thigh rises out and up from the hip and the knee is the highest point, a little above the lip, pointing up and back. The shin drops from the knee to the foot. Knee about 115°.
   - Hips and torso: hips at the lip, against the rock and square to it. The other leg hangs straight.
5. **La Fabrique Verticale, how to mantel** (`lafabriqueverticale.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/climbing-technique-how-mantle.jpg`). Fontainebleau sandstone, from above and behind. This is later: both palms are pressing.
   - Leg: the right leg is on the top out to the side. The knee is up and points up and out, bent about 90°, and the foot is on the top a thigh length out with its toes out.
   - Hips: at the lip, close to the rock, open towards the leg.
6. **climbinghouse.com, mantle article** (`climbinghouse.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/mantle_climbing_1.png`). Sandstone ledge, from behind and to the left. This is later: the foot is close to the hand.
   - Heel and foot: the right heel and foot are on the lip at hip height, just outside the right hand. The foot is pointed with the toes out to the side and the outside of the shoe towards the camera.
   - Leg: the knee is high, at shoulder height, pointing up, and bent about 50°. The shin drops steeply to the foot.
   - Hips: close to the wall and turned a little towards the foot.
7. **YouTube "Heel Hook Mantle", clip of Alex Tsway** (video 3Q9p_W14N-A, storyboard stills at about 0:18–0:24). Gym wall with a rail, from behind, at low resolution.
   - Heel: right heel on the rail just outside the right hand, at chest height.
   - Leg: the knee is up above the rail and points up. The thigh rises from the hip and the shin drops to the heel, knee about 70°.
   - Hips: close to the wall. This is a mantle onto a rail rather than a top, but the leg is shaped like the late frames above.

### Heel at or above head height, from behind

8. **climbinghouse.com, mantle article** (`climbinghouse.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/bouldering_top-719x1024.jpg`). Overhanging lip, from behind and above. The closest view to the book's.
   - Heel: right heel on the lip edge at shoulder height, about two forearms outside the right hand.
   - Thigh: nearly level, about 10° up from the hip. The knee is at chest height and points up and back.
   - Shin: carries on the same line to the heel, knee about 165°.
   - Foot: ankle about 115°, toes up and a little out over the edge. The outside of the shoe, with its strap, faces the camera and the back of the heel is on the edge.
   - Hips and torso: hips under the hands and close in, square to the rock. The other leg hangs straight down.
9. **climbinghouse.com, mantle article** (`climbinghouse.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/bouldering_mantle_climbers-776x1024.jpg`). Granite face, from behind and to the right, with a spotter.
   - Heel: left heel at the height of the left hand, about a forearm and a half outside it, on a hold.
   - Leg: one nearly straight line rising about 45° from the hip to the heel. The knee is at about 170° and points up and back.
   - Foot: pointed, about 130°, toes up and out. The outside of the shoe faces the camera.
   - Hips and torso: hips close to the rock, the torso upright and a little turned towards the heel. The other leg is straight down on a foothold.
10. **Alamy stock photo 2C8A19P**, captioned as a heel hook to get to the top of a boulder (`c8.alamy.com/comp/2C8A19P/...`, watermarked preview). Granite face, from behind.
    - Heel: left heel on an edge at shoulder height, about a forearm outside the shoulder.
    - Leg: tightly bent. The thigh is nearly vertical with the knee up at chest height pointing up, and the shin runs level out to the heel. Knee about 60°.
    - Foot: the toes point up and out.
    - Hips and torso: hips close in and turned towards the heel.
    - This shows the tight version of the hook, not the long leg of a hang from the lip.
