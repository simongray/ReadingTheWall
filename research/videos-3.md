## Method

**Transcripts.** YouTube's timedtext endpoint now returns an empty body unless the request carries the player's own token, and the transcript panel's request failed with a 400 in a logged-out browser. So I opened each video in the app's browser pane, let the player load its English auto-captions (after skipping the ad where there was one), and read that caption track back from the page. Every transcript is YouTube's machine captioning (ASR), so names and some words are garbled. I paraphrase it below and quote nothing.

**Other evidence.** Chapters, description, length, views and upload date come from the public watch page. Every URL went through `https://www.youtube.com/oembed?format=json&url=<URL>` on 2026-11-07 and returned 200, with the title and channel listed below. I read top comments for one video (Drop knee) and storyboard stills for one (the bumping candidate), stored in the session scratchpad. Time ran out before I could do more of either.

**Evidence labels.** Each verdict is marked:

- **[T]**: it rests on the transcript, plus chapters and the description.

The author should still watch every pick before release, since a transcript can't show what the climber's body does.

**Count.** 18 of the 21 picks rest on a transcript [T]. The other three (Pinch, Wrapping, Re-gripping) rest on chapters [C]. Of the new candidates, 10 videos were checked through transcripts.

### bicycle: keep [T]

### heel-toe-cam: keep [T]

### compression: keep, weak [T]

Climbing Tech Tips, "Climbing Movement: 16. Opposition vs. Compression | Climbing Tech Tips", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ar06kKAHmuA (2:04, 2018, 5.5k views). Chapters: 0:20 Compression, 0:50 Holds in pairs, 1:13 Bicycle.

A heel hook can stand in for one of the pair. The second minute covers toe hooks in a roof, the bicycle, chimneys and a deep drop knee, all filed under opposition.
- **Contradictions.** None, but the video blurs compression and opposition, as the book also does by listing the bicycle under both. It is narrated over stills and short clips from a small channel. It is acceptable until something better turns up.

### double-dyno and lunge: keep both, with a timestamp at 2:02 [T]

Mani the Monkey, "One-Hand or Double Dyno? Analytic Bouldering", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VSF6gUhP4WM (5:37, 2019, 13k views).

- **Evidence.** The first two minutes are a channel intro and his definitions: a dyno has both hands off at some point, a dynamic move doesn't. From about 2:02 he compares a one-handed and a double dyno on one steep problem. His rule of thumb weighs three things:
  - the quality of the target: a poorer hold calls for both hands;
  - distance and direction: the one-hand version reaches further, by a shoulder's width, and a sideways jump makes the far hand travel further in a double;
  - build: short arms with wide shoulders favour the one-hand version.
- **Contradictions.** None.
  - The double-dyno entry's "a target too poor to hold one-handed through a swing" is his first criterion.
  - His one-hand dyno is the lunge entry's "full form" (both feet off, one hand catches). The entry's basic form, with one foot or hand staying on, is not covered.
- **Note.** The two entries still share one video. `&t=122s` suits both.

### foot-catch (Jumping to a foothold): keep, optionally from 0:40 [T]

Bouldering Bobat, "How to Dyno || Intermediate Climbing Techniques || ToeHook catch", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=66m8HVOBqdo (4:24, 2017, 65k views).

- **Evidence.** After an intro story, he shows the move failing without the toe catch and then working with it (0:40). He splits it into launch, transition and catch, with hand and toe arriving together, then tensing the core with the support leg straight. Details from about 3:00:
  - get as much of the shoe's front behind the hold as possible;
  - flex the toes up;
  - keep the leg straight and drop the hips into it.

  Reading the route, so you see that a hold is meant for the toe, is called the biggest problem. That echoes the entry's "plan the foot before you move".
- **Contradictions.** None. The entry's "pull your toes up towards your shin" matches.

### comp-moves (Lache): keep, with a timestamp at 0:38; check one cue [T]

Felix Jockenhövel, "How to master the Lache Dyno", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dXUallpyVBE (4:35, 2024, 5.3k views). Chapters: 0:38 Momentum, 2:24 Direction, 3:56 Videos from Magic Wood?.

- **Evidence.** Two tips.
  - Momentum: his mistake in a competition was keeping his arms bent. He pulls in as the feet travel towards the target, then straightens the arms on the swing back to build momentum.
  - Direction: swing exactly towards the target. If the swing drifts off line, stop and start again, because you can't correct it in the air.

  Everything after 3:33 is channel talk.
- **Contradiction to check.** The entry says to hang with the arms slightly bent. The video says bent arms kill the swing and teaches straightening them on the backswing. The two can be reconciled (slightly bent, not locked), but a reader might see a conflict. The entry has no "stop and reswing" tip, which the video argues for.

### drop-knee: keep, with a timestamp at 0:56 [T, comments]

Movement for Climbers, "What the Heck is a Drop Knee?", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U0v9xxqEjfY (8:10, 2020, 111k views). Chapters: 0:56 How to Drop Knee, 1:38 Hip In, 2:58 Lateral Force, 4:40 Creating Torque, 5:51 Why Drop Knees are "Bad".

- **Evidence.** The drop-knee leg is on the same side as the moving hand. The foot pivots with an internal rotation until toe and knee point down. Then come three effects:
  - hip in, so the hips no longer sag on steep ground;
  - lateral force, which he shows on an overhung V6 with sloping footholds;
  - torque, a twist borrowed from MMA coaching.

  He then explains that the internal rotation loosens the knee's ligaments, so drop knees carry an injury risk that depends on knee health and load.
- **Contradictions.** None. Two things are worth knowing:
  - He calls the backstep the end position of a drop knee without the twist. The book treats them as separate entries, which is compatible.
  - The second-most-liked comment disputes the torque section, and another adds two cues: keep foot and knee aligned, and start the rotation from hip and heel.

### twist-lock: keep, optionally from 1:08 [T]

Neil Gresham Climbing Masterclass - Crux Films, "6 Neil Greshams Masterclass - Twistlock", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v4TnitXGxkM (3:23, 2016, 170k views).

- **Evidence.**
  - Square hips on steep ground overwork the arms.
  - Twisting the hips in gains reach without bending the arms (1:08).
  - Exaggerating the twist and rolling the body over locks the lower arm in place (1:30), which he compares with a figure-of-four.
  - A route sequence follows, then the claim that bent arms restrict forearm circulation and pump you faster.
- **Contradictions.** None. The video stresses straight arms. The entry has the holding arm pulled down to the ribs, which is the end of the lock, so the two describe different moments of the move. It is filmed on a steep sport route, not a boulder.

### pocket: keep [T]

Hooper's Beta, "What No One Tells You About Climbing On Pockets (Pocket Injury)", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3dd_STU3ps8 (8:16, 2021, 42k views). Chapters: 0:44 Strong-but-Risky, 3:09 Safe-but-Weaker, 3:57 Alternatives, 5:02 Training, 6:33 Outro and Bloopers.

- **Evidence.**
  - The risky grip curls the free fingers into a fist to gain force through the shared tendon (the quadriga effect), which risks the lumbricals.
  - The safe grip keeps the first segments of all fingers roughly parallel.
  - The alternatives are pressing through all four fingers and stacking fingers, with a caution about torsion.
  - Then comes basic pocket training.
- **Contradictions.** None. It is the entry's central warning, explained at more length. The last 1:40 are bloopers.

### cutting-loose: keep [T]

Mercedes Pollmeier of Modus Athletica, "How to Control The Swing In Climbing Movement | Tip Tuesday", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SUtyz4H-QEg (3:24, 2021, 5.3k views).

- **Evidence.** It starts on a pull-up bar (0:21) to show that a shorter lever swings less. Bend the elbows and tense the body as you swing, and let the back leg lead. On an overhanging wall (from 1:45):
  - swing with one foot on and feel where the swing stops;
  - switch legs at that point;
  - finally couple the switch with a dynamic catch.
- **Contradictions.** None. "Bend the elbows" matches the entry's slightly bent arms, and the shortened lever matches its tucked knees.
- **Alternative for a second slot.** Gresham's "18 Neil Greshams Masterclass - Cutting Loose", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UXNzqI4ohxU (1:01) [T]. On poor holds, don't cut both legs at once; cut one first and use body tension. The entry doesn't have that tip, but nothing contradicts it.

### hip-pump: keep, with a timestamp at 0:40; check one cue [T]

Movement for Climbers, "How the Pros Generate So Much Power", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nw4jPNl89qs (6:26, 2026, 12k views). Chapters: 0:40 The Underlying Principle, 3:03 The Application.

- **Evidence.**
  - Power comes from a launch position with about a 90° bend in the pushing leg.
  - You reach that position by weight transfer: moving the centre of mass over the pushing foot before jumping.
  - It compares two competitors on one dyno, then gives three steps on Charlie Barron's dyno: find the launch position, pull into it, jump the moment you arrive.

### overhang (Overhangs): keep [T]

Mani the Monkey, "How to Climb Overhangs: Techniques + Skills", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FPrdTd7tMdc (8:35, 2017, 407k views, manual English captions). Chapters: 1:32 Back step, 2:44 Hooks, 3:30 Climbing over edges, 4:35 Core strength, 5:56 Upper body and finger power.

1. Boulder Lab, "Bouldering technique: the BUMP! Learn how with Crack the Boulder", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zSY24Vlfp7Q (1:44, 2023, 1.6k views) [T, stills]. Chapters: 0:15 When to bump, 0:25 Power base, 0:51 Hip movement.
   - Bumping a hand up to the next hold is often the easiest option but easy to miss. It is powerful, so drive it from the hips and a leg: sink low, then move.
   - Practise on jugs first.
   - The stills (storyboard frames 25–49) show an indoor overhang with drawn arrows. They are too small to show whether the hand touches an intermediate first, and the transcript never says so.
   - No contradictions. Its stress on hip drive is something the entry doesn't mention.
   - A small gym channel.
2. Mercedes Pollmeier of Modus Athletica, "Bumping Hand Holds | Climbing Strength & Technique", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8AXT73CXMn8 (0:21) [D]. A drill clip.

### Lock-off: two usable candidates

1. beta boi brandon, "BEGINNER Lock Off MISTAKES I Wish I'd Fixed Sooner", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=boWxiknzEgU&t=90s (6:58, 2023, 59k views) [T]. This is the candidate videos-2 thought too weak, but its transcript is better than its title.
   - From 1:30 it defines the lock-off (pull, then hold the arm, usually at about 90°) and weighs pros and cons. The pros: static, precise on bad holds.
   - Tip 1 (3:54): push with the legs by extending the leg to lift the hips, or pull with a toe-hooked foot, so the arms do less.
   - Tip 2 (5:15): lead with the lats rather than the biceps, pulling the shoulder blades down and back.
   - Both tips match the entry ("let your legs push you up into the lock", "pull your shoulder blade down").
   - **Difference.** The video sets the lock at about 90° at the elbow. The entry brings the hand close to the shoulder, where the lock is easier, and keeps the elbow by the ribs, which the video doesn't mention. Not a contradiction, but worth a look.
2. Max Masteller, "THE SECRET to the LOCK OFF | Climbing tips", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ygHMWAl9MH8 (1:12, 2025, 11.5k views) [T]. A drill with two parallel handholds and one foothold: start low with straight arms, pull up slowly and reach as high as possible statically, with the legs doing most of the work of holding the lock. Short and on point, but from a small channel. No contradictions.

The same channel's "Intermediate Lock Off MISTAKES" (07A60aWW6m0) has a chapter on a "row motion" that may conflict with the entry's elbow-down cue. Not checked.
