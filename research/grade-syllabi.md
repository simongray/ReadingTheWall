# Grade syllabi: published schemes that place techniques by grade

Research note for the third edition, 2026-11-07. It tests the claim in
`FOLLOW-UPS.md` that "no public syllabus ties techniques to V-grades". It
compares every published skill scheme I could find with the grade badges in
chapters 3–7. No chapter or source file was changed. New sources are named
here for the author to add if he wants them. All pages were accessed on
2026-11-07.

## Verdict

- **The sentence no longer holds as written.** One national scheme ties
  named bouldering techniques to levels and ties each level to a bouldering
  grade: the French federation's (FFME) *passeports escalade*. Its grades are
  French bouldering grades, not V-grades. The book's own conversion table
  (Mountain Project and Wikipedia, F22 and F23) turns them into V-grades.
- **No scheme states V-grades for techniques.** That part of the claim is
  still literally true. The US gym clinics already cited (Touchstone,
  Movement) name V-grade bands, but they are course pages, not syllabi.
- **Everything else I checked ties techniques to levels, ages or nothing.**
  The UK, German, Austrian, Swiss and North American schemes and the research
  literature give no technique-to-grade link (details below).
- **The FFME places techniques earlier than the book, never later.** Each
  passeport lists what a climber learns at that level ("J'apprends à"). That
  matches the book's "learn it from" suggestions better than its badges,
  which mark where a technique starts to matter. Where the two disagree by two
  grades or more, the FFME is always the earlier one.

Suggested replacement for the `FOLLOW-UPS.md` bullet (the author's wording
kept where possible):

> **Grade placements** of most techniques are judgement calls. Only one
> public syllabus, the French federation's passeports, ties techniques to
> grades, and only through French bouldering grades (`research/grade-syllabi.md`).
> Revisit them as you climb more problems in your gyms.

The same claim appears in `book/chapters/04-body.typ` line 7 ("No syllabus
fixes these grades"), `book/chapters/00-front.typ` line 125, and in
`research/A-feet.md` line 21, `research/C-hands.md` line 1322 and
`research/F-grades.md` line 7. The 04-body sentence can stay, since no scheme
*fixes* the grades, but the author may want to soften it.

## 1. Schemes found

### 1.1 FFME passeports escalade (France): ties techniques to grades

- **Publisher:** Fédération française de la montagne et de l'escalade
  (FFME), the French climbing federation. Clubs and centres run the sessions
  and a qualified instructor validates each level.
- **Syllabus page:** https://www.ffme.fr/escalade/formation-escalade/passeports/
  (lists every module and its skills).
- **Grade evidence:**
  - The FFME page and the Hauts-de-France league page
    (https://hdf.ffme.fr/formation/passeports/) give each passeport a band,
    labelled "Niveau" on the league page: blanc 3a–4b, jaune 4a–5b,
    orange 4c–5c, vert 5a–5c, bleu 5c–6b, violet 6a–6c, rouge 6b–7a,
    noir 7b–8a.
  - The FFME's comparison of its professional courses (2022,
    https://www.ffme.fr/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/tableau_comparatif_formation_V02.pdf)
    treats a passeport as equal to a set of tests. Bleu equals two routes at
    6b and one boulder at 5c. Violet equals two routes at 6c and one boulder
    at 6a. Rouge equals routes at 6c and 7a and a boulder at 6b (men's
    standard; 6a for women).
  - The Hauts-de-France league's 2025–2026 course brochure
    (https://hdf.ffme.fr/uploads/2025/09/plaquette-formations-2025-2026.pdf)
    gives "5c en voie et 5a en bloc (passeport vert)" and says the bleu
    passeport attests 6b on routes and 5c on boulders. Its gym-instructor
    course asks for 5c on routes and 4c on boulders. The league page says
    the orange passeport gives access to that course.
  - So **the lower number of each band is the bouldering grade and the upper
    number is the route grade.** Four passeports confirm this directly (vert,
    bleu, violet, rouge) and orange through two statements taken together.
    For blanc, jaune and noir I read it from the same pattern.
- **Translation to V-grades** (bouldering grade, then the book's table in
  `a-grades.typ`, which follows F22 and F23):

  | Passeport | Bouldering grade | V-grade | Basis |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Blanc | 3a | VB | band pattern |
  | Jaune | 4a | V0 | band pattern |
  | Orange | 4c | V0 (V0+) | instructor prerequisite + league page |
  | Vert | 5a | V1 | league brochure |
  | Bleu | 5c | V1–V2 | league brochure, FFME table |
  | Violet | 6a | V3 | FFME table |
  | Rouge | 6b | V4 | FFME table (men) |
  | Noir | 7b | V8 | band pattern |

  The tables list Font 5 (V1) and 5+ (V2) but not 5a–5c, so I give bleu as
  V1–V2 and count it as V2 when I compare. The book notes that charts
  disagree by up to a full grade at the low end (F25). Read every low-grade
  placement below as ±1.
- **Techniques by level** (bouldering module unless marked; French terms in
  quotes):
  - **Blanc (VB):** moving in every direction, including traversing; pushing
    with the legs, not pulling; easy downclimbing; landing small falls.
  - **Jaune (V0):** using a slanted ("oblique") hold and reaching a far one;
    pushing on one or two legs; foot swaps ("changer de pied"), hand swaps,
    crossing hands ("croiser les mains"); catching a hold on the fly
    ("attraper une prise au vol").
  - **Orange (V0):** on steeper ground, oppositions on vertical handholds
    with slanted footholds; big-span moves ("mouvements d'amplitude");
    controlling barn doors ("portes") and sideways off-balance; loading small
    footholds; *exploring* hands-free dynamic moves ("skate mouv'") and
    balance moves on volumes; reading the problem and looking for hidden
    holds. The route module adds climbing straight-armed and side-on ("bras
    tendus, de profil") in steep sections, and smears.
  - **Vert (V1):** outdoor bouldering: smears, small edges and bumps;
    cracks, flakes, corners and ledges.
  - **Bleu (V1–V2):** dynamic moves, controlled and thrown ("conduits,
    jetés"); big moves such as foot-on-hand ("pied-main") and a rock onto a
    knee ("bascule sur un genou"); delicate placements ("griffé, spatule,
    crochet, assis-talon"); keeping rhythm through intense sequences;
    working out the sequence before trying.
  - **Violet (V3):** moves outside push-and-pull: pulling on a heel ("tirer
    sur un talon"), turning a handhold into a push, complex oppositions,
    "run and jump"; very intense short efforts.
  - **Rouge (V4):** unusual positions such as the body horizontal; complex
    trajectories; using off-balance to move; better grips; keeping weight on
    the feet at high intensity.
  - **Noir (V8):** performance only: ten boulders at the level, or a top-26
    national ranking.
- **Doubts:**
  - "Conduits" and "jetés" are the French pair for controlled dynamic moves
    and full throws. I read "conduits" as deadpoint-style moves.
  - "Crochet" means a hook, heel or toe. "Pied-main" is a foot placed where a
    hand was, which the book calls a hand–foot match.
  - I could not confirm "griffé", "spatule" or "assis-talon" in any glossary.
    "Griffé" may mean clawing with the toes. I leave all three out of the
    table.
  - The scheme covers routes and outdoor rock as well as gyms. Vert is an
    outdoor level.

### 1.2 French ministry levels: grades without techniques

- The baccalauréat physical-education tests score lead climbs by route grade
  (about 4a–7a), and the *académies* publish their own scales. Examples: Lyon
  "escalade bac pro n4 2021"
  (https://eps.enseigne.ac-lyon.fr/spip/IMG/pdf/escalade-bacpro-n4-2021.pdf),
  Bordeaux "Escalade Bac GT Niveau 5"
  (https://ent2d.ac-bordeaux.fr/disciplines/eps/wp-content/uploads/sites/33/2017/08/Escalade-Bac-GT-Niveau-5-Session-2019.pdf).
- They tie route grades to marks, not bouldering techniques to grades. I read
  them only through search summaries.
- The eduscol document I opened (https://eduscol.education.fr/document/50600/download)
  is a safety protocol with no levels.

### 1.3 UK: NICAS / NIBAS, the ABC, Mountain Training

- **NICAS Bouldering** (https://nicas.co.uk/?p=9518), run by the ABC Training
  Trust, which is linked to the Association of British Climbing Walls (ABC).
  It has five levels, from New Boulderer to Performance Boulderer. Level 2 adds
  route reading and further movement, and level 3 adds hold types and
  "advanced movement". The page names no techniques and no grades (re-checked;
  same as F28–F31). I found no separate public NIBAS syllabus.
- **Mountain Training, Bouldering Wall Instructor** skills checklist (June
  2025, https://www.mountain-training.org/media/i1pl5mby/bouldering-wall-instructor-skills-checklist-june-25.docx).
  Candidates must boulder at "minimum V2". They teach novices six "basic
  bouldering movement skills": centre of gravity, balanced movement with
  accurate feet, using the skeleton to hold the body's weight, contact with
  holds, safe falling, and safe topping out. That is a ceiling, not a
  placement: the basics belong at or below V2.
- **BMC / Mountain Training FUNdamentals 1–3** (B1–B3): levels, no grades,
  except that FUNdamentals 3 coaches should boulder V3/f6A+ (B3).

### 1.4 Germany, Austria and Switzerland: no grade ties

- **DAV Indoor Boulder-Check 1 and 2**
  (http://www.alpenverein.de/artikel/boulderschein_fc291e2e-f167-4505-9875-d141f21132ec):
  behaviour, rules, falling and the fall zone. No techniques and no grades
  (re-checked; same as F32).
- **ÖAV Kletterscheine:** I found only toprope, lead and outdoor licences,
  with no bouldering technique list. One Naturfreunde course asks for
  "6B bloc" as an entry level, which is a prerequisite and not a syllabus.
- **SAC and the Swiss federal sports office:** the SAC youth Gecko Trophy
  brochure has no grades. The mobilesport.ch school levels A, B and C ask for
  a boulder traverse, then roped routes "im Schwierigkeitsgrad 5" and "6",
  with no named scale and no techniques (search summary; the PDF link was
  dead).

### 1.5 North America: levels by age or team, no grades

- **USA Climbing:** I found no public coaching curriculum.
- **AMGA Climbing Wall Instructor**
  (https://www.amga.com/programs/climbing-instructor-programs/climbing-wall-instructor-program):
  prerequisites and "basic" movement skills, with no technique-by-grade list
  (search summary).
- **Climbing Escalade Canada, Athlete Development Matrix** (2021,
  https://climbingcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/CEC_Athlete-Development-Matrix-ADM-v1-2021.pdf;
  I read the copy Climbing New Zealand reposts at
  https://www.climbing.nz/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Athlete-Development-Matrix.pdf).
  It marks about 30 techniques as introduced, developed, consolidated or
  refined at each age from under 6 to 18+. Examples: heel and toe hooks,
  flagging and deadpointing are introduced at 6–8, and rockovers and the
  bicycle at 9–10. It gives an order by age, not by grade, so it has no
  column in the table.
- **Movement team tryout metrics**
  (https://movementgyms.com/app/uploads/sites/28/2023/11/Team-Tryout-Metrics.pdf):
  Intro, Intermediate, Advanced and Comp teams. Flagging, drop knees, heel and
  toe hooks, stemming, crossing through and rocking over are Intermediate;
  coordination moves and mantling are Advanced. No grades.

### 1.6 Gym clinics that name V-grades (already in the sources)

These are the "technique clinics" the front matter mentions. They give V-grade
bands but describe courses, not syllabi.

| Source | Band | Techniques |
|---|---|---|
| Touchstone "Intermediate Technique" (F33) | V3+ | footwork, momentum, hand positions |
| Touchstone "Gateway to Advanced Technique" (A42, B64, C46, D36, F34) | V5–V6 | drop knees, heel and toe hooks, rockovers and mantles, dynamic movement, dynos |
| Movement "Intermediate Technique Series" (A43, B63, F35) | "V5 plateau" | heel and toe hooks, flagging, body tension, compression, deadpoints, sequencing |
| River Rock "Intermediate Movement" (F36) | stuck at V2, V2–V5 styles | no list |
| Bouldering Project grade tiers (F27) | V4–V6, V7–V8 | "more powerful", "advanced movements" |

A clinic for climbers stuck at V5 shows that a technique matters by V5. It is
an upper bound, not the grade where the technique first appears.

### 1.7 Coaching books and research: nothing grade-tied found

- **Coaching material:** Power Company's movement framework and the
  "Advanced Climbing Technique" handout tie nothing to grades. The books
  already cited give a few grade remarks of their own, such as Rowland on the
  reverse flag below V4 (B44), but no progression.
- **Research:** I found no study that counts which moves appear at which
  recreational grades. The closest are:
  - a 2025 IEEE benchmark that detects heel hooks, toe hooks and dynos in
    bouldering video, with no breakdown by grade (search summary);
  - MoonBoard grade-prediction studies, such as
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11881084/, which model holds,
    not moves;
  - analyses of World Cup boulders, such as Augste et al. 2021 and E6, which
    cover elite grades only.

## 3. Grade notes that could cite a source

New source IDs are placeholders: **FFME** for the passeport page, plus the
FFME table or league brochure for the grade equivalence, and **BWI** for the
Mountain Training checklist. Each goes in the chapter's own yml. Only the
marked part of each note changes. Where the label covers a claim the source
does not support, I keep the label and add a sentence.

The strongest first:

1. **Run-and-jump** (`06-dynamic.typ`, `run-and-jump`). The violet bouldering
   module names it in English, and violet's bouldering grade is 6a, or V3.
   - Now: `Learn it from V3 on big holds (this book's suggestion).`
   - Suggested: `Learn it from V3 on big holds. France's climbing federation teaches the run and jump at that grade #src("FFME").`

2. **Heel hook** (`03-feet.typ`, `heel-hook`). This note has no label, but
   the match is exact: pulling on a heel is a violet skill (V3).
   - Now: `Climbers on one forum report that heel hooks become necessary around V3 #src("A44", "A45").`
   - Suggested: add after it `France's climbing federation teaches pulling on a heel at about the same grade #src("FFME").`

3. **Hand–foot match** (`03-feet.typ`, `hand-foot-match`). "Pied-main" is a
   bleu skill (5c, V1–V2).
   - Now: `It is the heart of a #tref("mantle", body: [mantle]), so you need it from V2 (judgement).`
   - Suggested: `It is the heart of a #tref("mantle", body: [mantle]), so you need it from V2. France's climbing federation teaches it at about that grade #src("FFME").`

4. **Deadpoint** (`06-dynamic.typ`, `deadpoint`). Controlled dynamic moves
   are a bleu skill (V1–V2). This rests on reading "conduits" as
   deadpoint-style moves.
   - Now: `Try small ones from V2 on big holds (this book's suggestion).`
   - Suggested: `Try small ones from V2 on big holds. France's climbing federation teaches controlled dynamic moves at about that grade #src("FFME").`
   - The "(judgement)" on V4–V5 stays.

5. **Dyno** (`06-dynamic.typ`, `dyno`). "Jetés" are a bleu skill (V1–V2). The
   double-dyno start is still the book's own idea, so its label stays.
   - Now: `Learn it from V2 on big holds, low on the wall, starting with a #tref("double-dyno", body: [double dyno], show-page: false) (this book's suggestion).`
   - Suggested: keep the sentence and add after it `France's climbing federation also teaches dynos from about V2 #src("FFME").`

6. **Precise footwork** (`03-feet.typ`, `precise-footwork`). Mountain
   Training lists "accurate use of the feet" among the basic skills a V2
   instructor teaches novices.
   - Now: `Learn it from your first session (this book's suggestion).`
   - Suggested: `Learn it from your first session. The UK's bouldering-instructor award lists accurate footwork among the basic skills for novices #src("BWI").`

7. **Latching** (`06-dynamic.typ`, `latching`). Catching a hold on the fly is
   a jaune skill (V0).
   - Now: `Learn it with your first deadpoints and dynos, on big holds (this book's suggestion).`
   - Suggested: `Learn it with your first deadpoints and dynos, on big holds. France's climbing federation teaches catching a hold on the fly from about V0 #src("FFME").`

8. **Barn door** (`04-body.typ`, `barn-door`). This supports the V1 badge.
   The label on "fixes 1 and 4" stays.
   - Now: `Hörst#ix("Hörst, Eric") teaches the cure, opposite hand and foot, as a basic #src("B11").`
   - Suggested: add after it `France's climbing federation teaches climbers to control barn doors at about V0 #src("FFME").`

9. **Roofs** (`07-terrain.typ`, `roof`). The rouge module (6b, V4) teaches
   positions with the body horizontal. It does not mention foot hooks, so the
   label stays.
   - Now: `Once the footholds face down or sideways, around V4–V5, your feet must hook to stay on (judgement).`
   - Suggested: add after it `France's climbing federation teaches climbing with the body horizontal at about V4 #src("FFME").`

10. **Mantle** and **Top-outs** (`05-hands.typ`, `mantle`;
    `07-terrain.typ`, `top-out`). Both say the heel hook "comes at V3". The
    violet module supports that. If item 2 goes in, these sentences can stay
    as they are, since the heel-hook entry carries the source.

Weaker candidates, not recommended unless the author wants more:
- **Smearing:** the FFME smears are in the route and outdoor modules.
- **Straight arms:** the source is the orange *route* module.
- **Turning the hip in:** the source says "de profil" in a route module, a
  level earlier than the book's suggestion.

## 4. Disagreements of two grades or more

In every case below, the FFME teaches the technique earlier than the book's
badge. In most of them the book's own note already says to learn it early. The
disagreement is then about what the badge means, not about the evidence:
- the passeport lists what a climber *learns* at a level;
- the badge marks where a technique starts to *matter* on gym problems.

| Technique | Book badge | FFME | Gap | The book's note already says |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dynamic slab | V5 | Orange (V0): *explore* hands-free dynamic moves | 5, loose | — (the source says only "explorer") |
| Latching | V4 | Jaune (V0): catching a hold on the fly | 4 | learn with your first deadpoints |
| Traverses | V2 | Blanc (VB): traversing | 3 | "Traverses appear at every grade" |
| Deadpoint | V3 | Jaune (V0) catch; Bleu (V1–V2) "conduits" | 1–3 | try small ones from V2 |
| Double dyno | V5 | Bleu (V1–V2): "jetés" | 3, loose | learn it from V2 |
| Overhangs | V3 | Orange (V0): "passages plus raides" | 3, loose | start on gentle overhangs from V2 |
| Opposition (simple) | V3 | Orange (V0) | 3 | hand-against-foot from V2 |

| One-handed dyno | V4 | Bleu (V1–V2): "jetés" | 2 | learn it from V3 |
| Toe hook | V4 | Bleu (V1–V2): "crochet" | 2, loose | a forum poster lists it from V3 |
| Foot swap | V2 | Jaune (V0) | 2 | — |
| Crossing hands | V2 | Jaune (V0) | 2 | — |
| Layback | V2 | Orange (V0) | 2 | — |
| Turning the hip in | V2 | Orange route module (V0) | 2 | try it from V1 |
| Side flag | V2 | Orange (V0): off-balance, flag not named | 2, loose | — |
| Volumes | V2 | Orange (V0): balance on volumes | 2 | — |

These are the 15 techniques from section 2, plus simple oppositions, which
section 2 counts under Opposition as agreeing.

Most worth a look:

- **Foot swap, crossing hands, layback and volumes** show the clearest
  conflict. The FFME names each one plainly at V0, and the book gives no
  "learn earlier" note. A reader at V0–V1 meets all four. The author may want
  a "learn it from V0" line or a lower badge.
- **Latching** is a conflict of meaning, not of evidence. The jaune skill is
  a small catch on big holds, and the book's V4 is about holding the swing
  after a long move.
- **Toe hook:** "crochet" covers heel and toe hooks. If the FFME means both at
  bleu, it disagrees with the book's V4. The gym clinics, which teach toe
  hooks to V5 climbers, still allow V4.

No source places any technique two grades or more *later* than the book.
